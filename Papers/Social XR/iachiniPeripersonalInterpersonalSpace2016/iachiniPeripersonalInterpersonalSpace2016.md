> [!abstract]-
> Proxemics and neuro-cognitive literature have suggested a close relationship between social and action functions of near-body space. Here we asked whether interpersonal-social (comfort distance) and peripersonal-action (reachability-distance) are similarly sensitive to social aspects. In an Immersive Virtual Reality (IVR) study (Exp. 1), participants determined reachability and comfort distances from virtual male/female children, young/old adults while standing still (passive) or approaching them (active). Exp. 2 assessed the ecological validity of the IVR-study by replicating real participant-confederate interactions. Exp. 3 used a paper-and-pencil proxemics measure to investigate spatial behaviors for practical/clinical purposes. Results showed that reachability and comfort distances were similar in active but not passive, conditions. Both distances were moderated by gender (reduction with females; expansion with males) and age (expansion with adults; reduction with children). These results were consistent among the three approaches. Therefore, interpersonal and peripersonal spaces share a common motor nature and are similarly sensitive to social aspects.

# 🧪Design & Methodologies

[[@iachiniPeripersonalInterpersonalSpace2016]] ([Page 2](zotero://open-pdf/groups/6528517/items/FUS6TQIL?page=155&annotation=XL5SHJUG))
> In Exp. 1, through the IVR system, participants had to determine [[reachability-distance]] ([[peripersonal action space]]) and [[comfort-distance]] ([[interpersonal social space]]) from male/female virtual confederates of different ages (children, young adults, elders) while standing still (passive) or walking toward them (active). 

[[@iachiniPeripersonalInterpersonalSpace2016]] ([Page 2](zotero://open-pdf/groups/6528517/items/FUS6TQIL?page=155&annotation=MGBJ8LET))
> In Exp. 2, participants had to determine reachability- and comfort-distance from actual male/female confederates under the active and passive approach conditions (similarly to Exp. 1). 

[[@iachiniPeripersonalInterpersonalSpace2016]] ([Page 2](zotero://open-pdf/groups/6528517/items/FUS6TQIL?page=155&annotation=YBM4PXAZ))
> Exp. 3 we used a paper-and-pencil task to investigate proxemics regulation. Participants determined their preferred interpersonal distances from males and females of different age, as in Exp. 1. This allowed comparison of the results for age and gender obtained in IVR with a classic proxemics measure. 

[[@iachiniPeripersonalInterpersonalSpace2016]] ([Page 7](zotero://open-pdf/groups/6528517/items/FUS6TQIL?page=160&annotation=IJX33PIF))
> in the passive-conditions participants were asked to: “stop the confederate as soon as the distance between you and the confederate makes you feel uncomfortable” (Passive Comfortdistance) and “stop the confederate as soon as you can reach the confederate” (Passive Reachability-Distance).
