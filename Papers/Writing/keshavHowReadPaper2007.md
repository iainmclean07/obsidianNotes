> [!abstract]-
> Researchers spend a great deal of time reading research papers. However, this skill is rarely taught, leading to much wasted effort. This article outlines a practical and efficient three-pass method for reading research papers. I also describe how to use this method to do a literature survey.

# ⭐Interesting Points

[[@keshavHowReadPaper2007]] ([Page ](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/PQRUTLSB?page=1&annotation=C8Z8TFIY))
> The f irst pass gives you a general idea about the paper. The second pass lets you grasp the paper’s content, but not its details. The third pass helps you understand the paper in depth. 

[[@keshavHowReadPaper2007]] ([Page ](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/PQRUTLSB?page=1&annotation=JF3TCI4W))
> 1. Carefully read the title, abstract, and introduction 2. Read the section and sub-section headings, but ignore  everything else 3. Read the conclusions  4. Glance over the references, mentally ticking off the  ones you’ve already read 

[[@keshavHowReadPaper2007]] ([Page ](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/PQRUTLSB?page=1&annotation=2GIAESZM))
> In the second pass, read the paper with greater care, but ignore details such as proofs. 

[[@keshavHowReadPaper2007]] ([Page ](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/PQRUTLSB?page=1&annotation=PX2365L9))
> 1. Look carefully at the figures, diagrams and other illustrations in the paper. Pay special attention to graphs. Are the axes properly labeled? Are results shown with error bars, so that conclusions are statistically significant? Common mistakes like these will separate rushed, shoddy work from the truly excellent. 2. Remember to mark relevant unread references for further reading (this is a good way to learn more about the background of the paper). 

[[@keshavHowReadPaper2007]] ([Page 2](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/PQRUTLSB?page=2&annotation=BZFFWJ2X))
> The key to the third pass is to attempt to virtually re-implement the paper: that is, making the same assumptions as the authors, re-create the work 

[[@keshavHowReadPaper2007]] ([Page 2](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/PQRUTLSB?page=2&annotation=L7YQDKF8))
> First, use an academic search engine such as Google Scholar or CiteSeer and some well-chosen keywords to find three to five recent papers in the area. 

[[@keshavHowReadPaper2007]] ([Page 2](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/PQRUTLSB?page=2&annotation=ETI6DUP7))
> if you are lucky, a pointer to a recent survey paper. 

[[@keshavHowReadPaper2007]] ([Page 2](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/PQRUTLSB?page=2&annotation=MD9T8EV5))
> If they all cite a key paper that you did not find earlier, obtain and read it, iterating as necessary. 
# 🧪Design & Methodologies

[[@keshavHowReadPaper2007]] ([Page ](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/PQRUTLSB?page=1&annotation=DSHDTM63))
> At the end of the first pass, you should be able to answer the five Cs:  1. Category: What type of paper is this? A measurement paper? An analysis of an existing system? A description of a research prototype? 2. Context: Which other papers is it related to? Which  theoretical bases were used to analyze the problem? 3. Correctness: Do the assumptions appear to be valid? 4. Contributions: What are the paper’s main contributions? 5. Clarity: Is the paper well written? 

[[@keshavHowReadPaper2007]] ([Page 2](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/PQRUTLSB?page=2&annotation=BJCWBSDR))
> The second pass should take up to an hour.
